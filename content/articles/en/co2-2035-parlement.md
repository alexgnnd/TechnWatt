On 4 November, the European Parliament's Environment Committee adopted, by **48 votes to 30** with two abstentions, a position that substantially alters the 2035 target for new cars. Where the current text required a 100% cut in CO2 emissions, the committee proposes **87.5%**. For light commercial vehicles, the target falls to 80%.

## What the figure means

A 100% reduction amounted to allowing only vehicles with zero tailpipe emissions from 2035. At 87.5%, the text leaves residual room for combustion engines, in reduced numbers, beyond that date.

Worth noting: the European Commission itself had proposed 90% in December 2025. The Environment Committee therefore goes further than the EU executive in easing the rules.

## Steel credits, the other mechanism

This is the least discussed part, and it matters. The text provides for credits for low-carbon steel produced in the Union, capped at 7.5 points. In other words, a manufacturer could meet part of its target not by selling lower-emission vehicles, but by sourcing decarbonised European steel.

Taking that cap into account, the effective target for cars rises back to 95%. The text also provides for super credits for electric vehicles built in the Union.

## Nothing is settled yet

The committee vote sets Parliament's negotiating mandate, and that mandate still needs approval from the full Parliament before any talks with member states can begin. That plenary vote is expected in **December 2026**. Negotiations with the Council would follow.

In other words: three stages still separate this text from applicable law.

## What it changes for a buyer

In the short term, nothing. Showroom stock, incentives and taxation do not depend on this vote. In the medium term it is another matter: a manufacturer anticipating an 87.5% obligation rather than 100% does not invest in its electric ranges at the same pace, and does not set its prices the same way.

## Our verdict

The debate is often framed as a referendum for or against electric cars. The figures tell a different story: the market is already shifting on its own, and it is the regulatory calendar adjusting to the industry's speed rather than the reverse. The point to watch is not the 2035 percentage, it is the steel credit mechanism — because a target you can meet by means other than selling clean cars is no longer really an automotive target.
