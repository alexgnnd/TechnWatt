Bidirectional charging has been talked about for years, usually in the future tense. At BMW it has been in the present tense since February, and it is now widening. On 18 November, the manufacturer and energy supplier E.ON announced that their Vehicle-to-Grid offer extends to the sixth-generation BMW i3, iX4 and iX5, joining the pioneering iX3.

## The principle, in one sentence

With Vehicle-to-Home, the battery powers the house. With **Vehicle-to-Grid**, it goes further: it also feeds back into the public grid. A car, parked most of the time, becomes an energy store that can be called on when demand rises — and that is the service the energy supplier buys.

## What it pays

Two mechanisms stack up.

The first is a **connection bonus**: 24 cents for every hour the car is plugged in and available. Over a year that comes to as much as **€720**. BMW translates the figure its own way: enough to cover around 16,000 kilometres free of charge in the new i3.

The second pays for the energy actually returned, at **42 cents per kilowatt-hour**.

In other words, you are paid for being plugged in, then paid again if the grid actually draws on the battery.

## What you need

Three things, all of them required: a compatible vehicle — iX3, i3, iX4 or iX5, all on the sixth generation of BMW eDrive technology — the bidirectional **BMW Wallbox Professional**, and the **E.ON ÖkoStrom Home & Drive V2G** electricity tariff.

Control is automatic: the system decides on its own when to charge and when to feed back. The driver simply sets the desired state of charge and the departure time in the My BMW app. The car is always ready when it needs to be.

## The catch

The offer is **German**. It does not yet cover France, and the reason is regulatory rather than technical: feeding back into the public grid requires a tariff framework and a contract to match, which French suppliers are only starting to offer.

## Our verdict

This is one of the rare charging announcements that genuinely changes the economics of a plug-in car. Until now, a battery was expensive and earned nothing between journeys. Here it becomes an asset that works while you sleep. The condition remains the hardware: the bidirectional wallbox and the specific contract are an investment to pay off. But at €720 a year the maths comes together quickly — and this is exactly the kind of offer we would like to see cross the Rhine.
