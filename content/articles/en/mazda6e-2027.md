Launched with two batteries making contradictory promises, the Mazda6e returns for 2027 on a far clearer footing. The brand drops its 68.8 kWh LFP / 80 kWh NMC pairing for a single 78 kWh LFP battery, and takes the opportunity to fix what irritated most: charging. First European deliveries are announced for this month of November.

## One battery, one power output

The motor produces 190 kW (258 hp) on the rear axle, whatever the trim. On the WLTP urban cycle, Mazda claims up to 703 km, with combined consumption of 16.1 kWh/100 km. The urban figure is flattering, as always, but the combined consumption puts the saloon among the better performers in its class — careful aerodynamics will do that.

The choice of LFP chemistry is not neutral: lower energy density, but better tolerance of repeated charges to 100% and a longer claimed service life.

## The real news: 200 kW

The old 6e peaked too low for a long-distance saloon. The new one accepts up to 200 kW on DC, which puts it back on terms with the segment benchmarks and genuinely changes how you string motorway legs together.

Regenerative braking has been strengthened and the suspension recalibrated, two tweaks aimed at the same goal: making the car more coherent between town and fast road.

## Smart Cruise Assist up to 160 km/h

On the driver-assistance side, Smart Cruise Assist now works up to 160 km/h. Navigation includes charging-point filtering, showing only the chargers that actually match the car and the power you are after — a feature that sounds secondary until the first pointless detour.

## Prices in France

- **Takumi:** €43,900
- **Takumi Plus:** €45,900
- **Homura Plus:** €45,900

Homura Plus is new for this model year, pitched at the same price as Takumi Plus with a sportier presentation.

## Our verdict

Mazda has done the most useful thing: clarified the range and fixed the charging. One battery, one power output, 200 kW DC — that is an offer you can explain in two sentences, which was not the case last year. What remains is to check the claimed combined consumption on the road, because that figure, not the 703 urban kilometres, will decide whether the 6e really goes the distance on a long trip.
