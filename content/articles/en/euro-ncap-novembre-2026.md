On 18 November, Euro NCAP published results for ten vehicles assessed under its 2026 protocols, the most demanding ever applied. Nine earn five stars. One stops at four. And one of them sets a benchmark nobody had reached before.

## The record: 94% for occupant protection

The Škoda Peaq holds it. With **94% in Crash Protection**, it posts the highest score yet recorded by any vehicle tested under the 2026 protocols. It adds 92% in Post Crash Safety, 78% in Crash Avoidance and 65% in Safe Driving.

That last figure is worth pausing on, and Euro NCAP does not let it pass.

## Protection is not everything

The programme now assesses four pillars: Safe Driving, Crash Avoidance, Crash Protection and Post Crash Safety. Programme Director Aled Williams points out that crashworthiness is only part of the picture, and that true all-round safety relies on striking a balance between these areas.

On that ground, the Mercedes-Benz GLE (74% and 87%) and BMW X5 (71% and 85%) set a higher bar than the Peaq in Safe Driving and Crash Avoidance. The Peaq takes full marks for manual driving and infotainment controls, but lacks Child Presence Detection and out-of-position occupant monitoring.

The Lepas L6 and L8 PHEV post the round's best Post Crash Safety score, at 95%.

## The only four-star car

The Nissan Leaf brings up the rear, lowest in all four pillars. Two omissions weigh heavily: no standard front corner radars, which hurts its crossing-traffic detection, and no standard centre airbag.

For a car now in its third generation, and one that long served as the flagbearer for affordable electric motoring, the result raises questions.

## Our verdict

Two lessons. First: crash protection is no longer the issue, with nine models out of ten clearing the bar comfortably. The second is more interesting — what now separates cars is the standard fitment of driver assistance and detection systems. An unticked option costs more points today than a less rigid structure.
