titre: '41% electric in France: a record… that makes Norway smile'

It is a figure to be proud of: in October 2026, 41.5% of new cars registered in France were fully electric. A year earlier, they accounted for just 24%. In a market up 7%, with 149,341 registrations, electric cars grew by more than 80% year on year.

## One new car in three since January

The trend did not start in October. Over the first ten months of the year, 449,303 electric cars were registered in France, 32.5% of the market. And in October, a small French car led the way: the Renault Twingo E-Tech electric, with 4,747 registrations, just ahead of the Renault 5.

## Meanwhile, in Norway…

Yet you only need to look north to put things in perspective. In Norway, 98.7% of new cars sold in August were electric, and 97.8% over the first eight months of the year. Combustion cars have become a curiosity there. Tesla remains the best-selling brand, with around 17.5% of registrations, but Chinese brands are growing fast: around one in four electric cars sold in the country comes from them.

## Denmark is far ahead too

Denmark is on the same path: more than eight in ten new cars there are electric in 2026. Across the European Union, the average remains much more modest, at 21.7% between January and August.

## The Nordic secret

There is no miracle recipe. Norway bet early on massive tax advantages for electric cars, heavy taxation of combustion cars and a charging network built even before the cars arrived in large numbers. The result: electric cars already make up almost a third of its car fleet, compared with a small fraction in France.

## Our verdict

France is catching up fast, driven by affordable models built in Europe. But Norway's example is a reminder that sales are not enough: it takes time to renew an entire fleet, and a charging network that keeps pace.
