Apple unveiled the iPhone Duo on 9 September 2026, and it reached stores on 23 October, France included. Closed, it is a compact iPhone with a 5.4-inch screen. Open, it becomes a 7.6-inch mini tablet. Both OLED displays run at up to 120 Hz.

## A 5.2 mm titanium foldable

The chassis is titanium and just 5.2 mm thick when open. The front is protected by Ceramic Shield 2, the hinge gets a 3D-printed cover, and the whole thing is IP68 water and dust resistant. Two colours are available: Night Sky and Star White.

## Touch ID instead of Face ID

To save space, Apple made some bold choices. No Face ID: you unlock with your finger, using Touch ID built into the side button, whether the phone is open or closed. On the back, two 48 MP sensors, wide and ultra-wide, but no telephoto like the iPhone 18 Pro. A clever touch: the outer screen shows the framing to the person you are photographing.

## A20 Pro, C2 and N1

Under the hood, the iPhone Duo shares the A20 Pro chip of the iPhone 18 Pro, built on a 2 nm process, with a 6-core CPU, a 7-core GPU and a Neural Engine for Apple Intelligence. A vapour chamber removes the heat. It also has the Apple C2 modem for 5G and the N1 chip for Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread. Note: it works with dual eSIM only, with no physical SIM slot.

## iOS 27 designed for two screens

Shipping with iOS 27, the Duo gets iPad-style multitasking: in landscape mode, Split View shows two apps side by side on the inner screen. Apple Pencil (USB-C) support has been announced but will arrive later in an update.

## Battery and charging

Each half of the phone hides its own battery. Apple claims up to 24 hours of battery life, wired charging up to 60 W through the USB-C port, and MagSafe and Qi2 wireless charging.

## Price

The iPhone Duo starts at €2,339 in France with 256 GB of storage, and goes up to 2 TB. Apple also offers two dedicated accessories: the iPhone Duo Case and the Duo Folio with a kickstand.

## Our verdict

Apple is late to foldables, but with a very polished device: record thinness, a cutting-edge chip and adapted software. The price remains for a small minority, though, and the lack of Face ID and telephoto is a reminder that you are paying for the format first.
