Some concept cars preview a model; others state an intention. This one is firmly in the second camp. To mark 50 years of the GTI, Volkswagen revealed the **ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept** on 20 November, an all-electric track study derived from the production ID. Polo GTI. The brief is crystal clear: anything that does not help it go faster has been removed.

## 367 PS through the front wheels

The concept uses the **APP550** motor, normally found on the rear axle of much larger models such as the ID.7 and ID. Tiguan. Here it sits at the front.

The result: **270 kW, or 367 PS**, against 166 kW and 226 PS for the production ID. Polo GTI. Torque rises to **550 Nm** instead of 290. Enough for 0-100 km/h in **4.7 seconds** with Launch Control, and a top speed of **240 km/h**.

Putting that much power through the front axle calls for a safeguard: that is the job of the electronically regulated **VAQ limited-slip differential**.

## A battery built to sprint

The **52 kWh** battery is not there for range but for output: it can deliver up to **290 kW** in brief peaks. Everything sits on the **MEB+** platform.

The adaptive **DCC** chassis has been recalibrated for the track, and the one-box braking system gets **ceramic discs**.

## The strict diet

Inside is where the stance hits hardest. **There is no infotainment system.** No central screen, nothing: Volkswagen has concentrated everything on ergonomics and driving.

Two seats, and only two: **Recaro** buckets with red five-point harnesses. The rear bench and the boot are gone, replaced by a **roll bar painted in Tornado Red** right behind the seats. Dashboard and trim are finished in dark ArtVelours microfibre with red topstitching — made partly by Volkswagen apprentices, a detail that says a lot about the in-house spirit.

![](img/clubsport-arriere.jpg)

## The bodywork follows

Gone are the production car's four doors: the concept is a **two-door**. The wings are flared by 50 mm on each side, the front splitter is motorsport-grade, the rear widens, a diffuser appears, and a **carbon-fibre wing** rises above the roofline.

## Where to see it

Public debut at the **Essen Motor Show**, from 27 November to 6 December 2026.

## Our verdict

Volkswagen had a problem to solve: convincing people that those three letters still mean something without a combustion engine. This study answers in the most direct way possible — by taking away rather than adding. No screen, no rear bench, no all-wheel drive to paper over the difficulty: front-wheel drive, a controlled differential, two buckets and a roll bar. It is the vocabulary of the historic GTIs applied to mechanicals that have nothing in common with them. Which leaves the question nobody is asking out loud yet: how many of these ingredients would survive the move to production?
