## How to fill it in properly

**The price per kWh** is the decisive variable. Take it from your bill, not from memory. Off-peak at home it sits around &euro;0.15 to &euro;0.22. On a motorway rapid charger, expect &euro;0.50 to &euro;0.70. If you alternate, use a weighted average: 80% at home and 20% on the road, for instance.

**Electric consumption** varies a lot with use. Expect 13 to 15 kWh/100 km for a city car in town, 17 to 19 for a compact in mixed use, 22 to 25 for an SUV on the motorway in winter. The WLTP figure is a floor, not an average.

**Petrol consumption**: use your real figure measured at the pump, not the manufacturer's. The gap is generally 15 to 25%.

## What this calculation does not say

It compares energy, and nothing else. Three further items come on top, and they all lean the same way:

- **Servicing**, clearly lower on electric: no oil changes, no belt, no clutch, and pads that last longer thanks to regenerative braking.
- **Taxation and incentives**, which vary with your situation and the delivery date.
- **Depreciation**, the great unknown, and the only item that can count against electric depending on the model.

Purchase price does not enter this calculation either: it is reasoned separately, as a monthly payment or total cost of ownership.

## The right reflex

Do the calculation twice. Once with your home price per kWh, once with a rapid-charger price. The gap between the two results will tell you, far better than any model comparison, whether electric suits your situation — and what a parking space with a socket is actually worth.
