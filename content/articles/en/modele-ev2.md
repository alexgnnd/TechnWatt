Built in Žilina, Slovakia, on Kia's E-GMP platform, the EV2 starts at €26,670 in Light trim. It borrows much of the technology of the brand's more expensive models, in a 4.06 m body designed for the city.

## Behind the wheel

Two characters. With the small battery, the EV2 gets 147 hp and does 0 to 100 km/h in 8.5 seconds, quite lively. The heavier big battery makes do with 135 hp and 9.5 seconds. In both cases, top speed is capped at 161 km/h and the simple beam rear axle favours compactness. Road holding at 120 km/h is described as reassuring, with enough in reserve to overtake calmly.

## Consumption

Rated at around 15.1 to 15.2 kWh/100 km depending on the battery, the EV2 often does better in town, sometimes under 12 kWh. In mixed use, count on 14 to 15.5 kWh/100 km, or about 300 km of real range with the small battery and more than 400 km with the large one. On the motorway in summer, expect around 20 kWh/100 km, more in winter.

## Screens and software

Three screens sit under a single pane of glass: two 12.3-inch displays with a small climate screen in between. There are two systems. The basic one covers the essentials at lower cost. The full one adds navigation with live traffic, an AI-powered voice assistant you summon with "Hey Kia", and the Kia Digital Key, stored in your phone's wallet, on iPhone or Android. The car updates over the air, free for seven years.

## Driver assistance

Kia has emptied the shelf: Highway Driving Assist, which handles speed and steering on the motorway, latest-generation adaptive cruise control, advanced emergency braking, Blind-Spot View Monitor, which shows the blind spot in the instrument display when you indicate, and a 360° view. Handy in a tight garage: with Remote Smart Parking Assist, you can move the car in or out from outside, using the key. The EV2 also debuts an interior camera at Kia that keeps an eye on the cabin.

## Materials and finish

A pragmatic feel: lots of hard plastics, lifted by softer materials where your hands rest. Kia highlights ten more responsible materials, including bio-based plastics and paints free of aromatic solvents. The floating console frees up space between the two front seats.

## Seating and boot

In five-seat form, the 362-litre boot is about average. The four-seat version, with a sliding split rear bench, takes it to 403 litres or gives more legroom, depending on the day's needs. A small 15-litre front boot holds the cables.

## Charging

Thanks to its 400 V architecture, the EV2 goes from 10 to 80% in around 30 minutes on a fast charger, whichever battery you choose. On alternating current, it accepts 11 kW as standard and 22 kW as an option, and supports Plug & Charge: plug in and charging starts, with no card or app.

Its real plus: it can give energy back. With V2L, up to 3.7 kW, you can plug in a coffee maker when camping or charge an e-bike. It is also V2G compatible, to support the grid at peak times.
