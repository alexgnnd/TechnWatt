It is a first for Germany: since 18 November, passengers can be offered an autonomous vehicle when booking a ride on the Uber app. The pilot, launched with China's Momenta, covers northern Munich, including the neighbourhoods around the Olympic Park. Rides are free, participants pre-selected, and a trained safety operator sits in every vehicle.

## What has actually changed

Not the technology — it has been running for years in China and the United States. What changed is the authorisation. Germany's Federal Motor Transport Authority, the KBA, granted Momenta Europe GmbH **Level 4** testing permits. In the KBA's list of approvals dated 5 November, the company holds three, and none carries a geographic restriction: the permit covers urban roads across Germany, with no separate application city by city.

That is the real shift. Until now, every European trial was an isolated case tied to a precise perimeter.

## Level 3, Level 4: the nuance that changes everything

In EU member states, only autonomous driving **up to Level 3** is currently authorised in normal traffic: the driver may sometimes let go of the wheel and pedals, but must stay constantly alert and take back control when the system asks. Mercedes-Benz's Drive Pilot on certain German motorways is one example.

**Level 4** is a different matter: the vehicle fully handles driving within its operational domain, expecting no human intervention. In Munich it is framed by a testing permit, an operator on board and free rides — an experiment, not a commercial service.

## The cars being used

The pilot uses the second generation of the IM LS6, a fastback crossover from the partnership between SAIC and Alibaba. The vehicles run Nvidia Thor processors and LiDAR hardware.

## What to expect

The Munich perimeter is due to expand in stages, and for the first participants, designated stops already determine which trips are actually possible. In other words: this is still a long way from a robotaxi you hail anywhere, anytime.

But Europe no longer merely has autonomous driving projects. It has autonomous cars carrying passengers, in a major city, under a national authorisation.

## Our verdict

The contrast with the United States is instructive. Over there, Tesla sells a vehicle with no steering wheel to private buyers and charges them a subscription to make it move. Here, progress comes through testing permits, an operator on board and free rides. It is slower, it is more tightly framed, and it is probably what will make these services acceptable when they start charging. The signal to watch is not the Munich zone expanding, but the date an equivalent pilot is authorised in France.
