At IFA in Berlin in September 2026, BenQ presented its first 4K triple laser projectors: the TK715i and the W4200. A real turning point for the Taiwanese brand.

## A new laser engine

Both models use a 0.47-inch DLP DMD chip, paired with Nichia's new Qualas 42 RGB triple laser engine. They are rated at 3,200 lumens and include an AI chip that optimises the picture in real time and helps with installation. BenQ adds its four-layer De-Speckle technology, which reduces the grainy shimmer typical of laser projectors.

## TK715i: for the living room and gaming

The TK715i replaces its predecessor's 4LED source with laser. It has the Google TV interface and claims input lag of just 5 ms in 4K at 60 Hz. It stays quiet, at 26 dBA in Normal mode and 24 dBA in Eco mode. Its 1.43x optical zoom and 0.98 to 1.4 throw ratio make installation easier. Indicative price: €1,799.

## W4200: for home cinema

The W4200 replaces the W4100i and W5800. Designed for purists, it does without Google TV to focus on the picture. It covers 99% of the DCI-P3 colour space, gets an iris, a new motorised glass lens and supports Dolby Vision and IMAX Enhanced. Each unit is factory calibrated, with a certificate in the box.

## Availability

Dates vary between announcements: the TK715i is expected between late 2026 and early 2027. French pricing for the W4200 has not yet been announced.

## Our verdict

By moving to triple laser, BenQ catches up with XGIMI and Hisense. The TK715i targets a broad audience with Google TV and low latency, while the W4200 is for film lovers who want a calibrated picture.
