Nearly one French motorist in two is considering replacing their combustion car with an electric one. That is the finding of the third edition of the OpinionWay barometer "The French and the electric vehicle", carried out for Electra. But between considering and buying there is a step — and that is what the second set of figures is about.

## 26%, and a steady rise

Among those surveyed, **26% say they want to buy an electric vehicle in the short or medium term**, a proportion up eight points in two years. It is the second consecutive year of growth.

Set against that, a reminder that restores the scale: electric vehicles still account for only 3% of cars on the road. Intent precedes action by several years, the time it takes for a car to be replaced.

## What motivates, what holds back

On motivations, four in ten of those concerned cite reducing their emissions. The barometer also records a more concrete signal: 71% of respondents say they are ready to go on holiday in an electric vehicle, whatever the distance. That is probably the most telling figure of the lot — it covers precisely the use sceptics consider impossible.

On the brakes, charging time is cited by 54% of respondents, up six points. It is not range that worries people first, it is the length of the stop.

## Diesel is falling fast

Another figure from the barometer says a great deal about the underlying shift: diesel's share of the car fleet fell from 53% in 2022 to 42% in 2024. Eleven points in two years.

That decline does not benefit electric alone — petrol and hybrid take most of it. But it shows one thing: the fleet is moving, and moving quickly.

## Our verdict

Public debate likes to set believers against refuseniks. The figures describe something more pragmatic: a population that has accepted electric as a serious option and is waiting for conditions, not arguments. The brake cited first — charging time — happens to be the easiest to lift: it is solved with more powerful, better distributed chargers, not with communication campaigns.
