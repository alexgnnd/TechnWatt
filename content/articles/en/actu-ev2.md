With the EV2, Kia moves one step down its electric range. This 4.06 m SUV slots in below the EV3 and starts at €26,670 in Light trim. Yet it borrows much of the technology of the brand's more expensive models.

## Made in Europe

The EV2 is built in Žilina, Slovakia, in the plant that already produces the EV4. The small-battery version came off the line first, followed by the long-range version. This European production is an asset in France, where purchase incentives take into account the carbon footprint of manufacturing.

## Two batteries, two motors

The Standard Range version pairs a 42.2 kWh battery with a 147 hp motor, for 317 km of WLTP range. The Long Range version moves to 61 kWh with a 135 hp motor, and climbs to 453 km. 0 to 100 km/h takes between 8.5 and 9.5 seconds depending on the version. The EV2 can also tow up to 750 kg.

## Charging

Built on a 400 V architecture, the EV2 goes from 10 to 80% in around 30 minutes on a fast charger, whichever battery you choose. On alternating current, it accepts 11 kW as standard and 22 kW as an option. It supports Plug & Charge, which starts charging as soon as the cable is plugged in, with no card or app, as well as battery preconditioning.

## Real versatility

The EV2 can power an electrical device thanks to V2L, up to 3.7 kW: enough for a coffee machine when camping or a tool on a building site. It is also V2G compatible. On the road, it offers a One Pedal mode and steering wheel paddles to adjust energy recovery. Over-the-air (OTA) updates are free for seven years.

## Six versions in the range

In France, the range starts at €26,670 for the EV2 Light 42.2 kWh. Next come the Air (€28,820) and Earth (€31,320) with the same battery. With 61 kWh, the Air starts at €33,320, the Earth at €35,320 and the sportier-looking GT-Line at €37,320. Depending on the version, the EV2 offers a panoramic roof, a wireless charger and Apple CarPlay.

## Our verdict

City-friendly size, big-model technology and real versatility: the EV2 ticks many boxes. Be careful with the small battery if you often drive on the motorway, though: at 130 km/h, even the 61 kWh version covers around 160 km between 20 and 80% charge.
