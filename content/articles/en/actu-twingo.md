Starting at €19,490 before incentives, the Renault Twingo E-Tech electric instantly ranks among the cheapest new electric cars on the market. Once the bonus is deducted, eligible households pay around €15,000. Renault developed it in under two years, a record for the brand, and builds it in Slovenia.

## A face a whole generation will recognise

Round headlights, a smiling grille, flared wings: the nod to the 1993 Twingo is deliberate. The newcomer is 3.79 m long, keeps five doors and focuses on versatility. The rear bench slides and folds 50/50, and the front passenger seat back folds flat depending on the version. Slide the bench back for more knee room; slide it forward and the boot grows to 360 litres.

## A Renault 5 platform, a brand-new battery

Underneath, the Twingo uses the front axle of the Renault 5 and Renault 4 E-Tech electric. At the rear, it gets a flexible axle derived from the Captur, simpler and cheaper. Another first: its 27.5 kWh battery uses LFP (lithium iron phosphate) chemistry, a first for Renault Group. Cheaper and known for its durability, it trades this for lower energy density.

The 60 kW (80 hp) E-Tech motor is not chasing outright performance. It covers 0 to 50 km/h in 3.8 seconds, which is what really matters in town. Top speed is limited to 130 km/h.

## 263 km, designed for everyday life

With 263 km of WLTP range and an official consumption of 12 to 13 kWh/100 km depending on the version, the Twingo is among the most efficient electric cars. Renault calibrated it for an average use of around 35 km a day: enough to last the week without charging. On the motorway, however, the range drops quickly.

## Charging: watch out for the Advanced Charge pack

As standard, the Twingo charges on alternating current at 6.6 kW, about 4 hours for a full charge on a wallbox. Fast charging is not included: you need the optional Advanced Charge pack. It adds an 11 kW bidirectional AC charger and DC charging up to 50 kW, for 10 to 80% in around 30 minutes. The pack also enables V2L, to power an electrical device from the car, and V2G, to send energy back to the grid.

## Two trims: Evolution and Techno

The Evolution trim covers the essentials: automatic emergency braking, sliding rear bench, air conditioning and a mode 3 charging cable. The Techno trim adds the OpenR Link system with Google built-in on a 10.1-inch screen, the Reno avatar, automatic climate control, the hands-free card, Stop & Go adaptive cruise control, a rear camera, One Pedal driving and a six-speaker Arkamys audio system. Depending on the version, the Twingo offers up to 24 driver assistance systems, which can be switched off in one go with the My Safety Switch button.

## Our verdict

A tight price, clever dimensions and equipment worthy of a higher class: the Twingo E-Tech electric ticks many boxes for city use. The real point to watch is the Advanced Charge pack: without it, you cannot stop at a fast charger. For anyone who regularly leaves town, it is essential.
